Reading it: "Python / kit" above 1 is the kit faster. The Velox SI row is the stream decode (RosettaSciIO: numba-jitted, warm). The MIB and MRC rows are byte-swap and copy at memory speed; MIB reads were 934 ms at the first measurement (see below).

Backend decision per function (D16)

Readers stay on the CPU: they are bound by file reads and memory copies, there is no arithmetic for a GPU to take over, and host-to-device copies of a freshly read array cost more than the read. Measured break-even: none exists, no Metal path is a work item for any reader. Threading and mmap: only the 4D loads above 100 MB could split a read over cores; at the typical size the 4D rows are already within 1.1x of h5py and of RosettaSciIO, so the question belongs to the nightly large rows (open-items).

FunctionBackendWhy
DM, TIFF, MRC, MIB, EMPAD, HDF5 4D, Velox images and spectra, MSACPU onlyread-bound; no per-element arithmetic beyond byte swaps
Velox spectrum-image decodeCPU onlysequential stream with data-dependent branches (markers); the kit is already 3.3x RosettaSciIO's numba loop at 128²

Slower than RosettaSciIO at the typical size

Listed first in docs/open-items.md with the cause. Found and fixed in this session (diagnosed with sample, then measured): MIB 934 ms to 35 ms (an ISO8601DateFormatter built per frame header, 1024 times, plus a per-pixel Data.append); MSA 111 ms to 17 ms (per-line allocation in the first reader).

ImagoKit 0.6.0

Full file and method

Run 2026-10-21 on the audit session (ImagoKit 0.6.0 sources). Header. Apple M5 Pro, macOS (Darwin 27.0.0), Swift 6.4 (swiftlang-6.4.0.34.1), swift test -c release; Python 3.12.14 from refs/skimage with NumPy 2.5.4, SciPy 1.18.1, scikit-image 0.26.0 (single-threaded np.fft/ndimage). Method. The same function, dtype and input size on both sides (uniform random pixels 30 to 70); one warm-up call, then five timed calls; the table shows the median and (min–max) in milliseconds. FFTs reuse their plan (FFT2D, FFT2DDouble; NumPy caches its own); rfft2, butterworth, the shifts, registrations and filters are the public one-call API. Caveat. The five other kit audits ran on the machine at the same time (load average 11 to 25 during these runs, 6 to 8 before): absolute times are noisy, and both sides were measured in the same minutes so the ratios are comparable but not exact. The sizes are the small (128²) and typical (512²) ones plus one non-power-of-two (500²); masked registration, which costs eleven transforms of (2n−1)², is at 64² and 256². Large sizes (2048², 4096², 8192²) are nightly: IMAGO_BENCH_LARGE=1 and --large add them; they were not run here.

Reproduce (under the suite's memory cap, one benchmark at a time machine-wide):

```sh

IMAGO_BENCH=1 IMAGO_BENCH_OUT=/tmp/swift.json ../tools/memcap.sh -- swift test -c release --filter ImagoBenchmarks ../tools/memcap.sh -- ../refs/skimage/bin/python -I -B tools/benchmarks/bench.py /tmp/python.json python3 tools/benchmarks/report.py /tmp/swift.json /tmp/python.json

```

The harness is Tests/ImagoBenchmarks/Benchmarks.swift (its one test returns at once unless IMAGO_BENCH=1); tools/benchmarks/bench.py is the Python side. "kit / Python" below 1 means the kit is faster.

What the rows compare, and what they do not

niggli_reduce: the kit's UnitCell.niggliReduced against pymatgen's Lattice.get_niggli_reduced_lattice (pure Python) and

spglib's C niggli_reduce; the kit is faster than both (the first Python row is pymatgen). Cells are built outside the timer.

reflections: not like for like. The kit's reflections(kMax:) also gives d, the absence flag, the rings and their

orbits; pymatgen's get_points_in_sphere returns the points only. The kit is 2.3 to 6.6 times slower on the points.

kinematic_reflections: the kit's kinematicReflections(kMax:retainingAll:false) against py4DSTEM's

Crystal.calculate_structure_factors on the same C2/m cell; the kit is 1.8 to 4.5 times faster. Both use the Lobato-Van Dyck table.

symmetry_detection and cif_import: the kit's CIFImport.read always detects the symmetry of the cell it reads;

pymatgen's CifParser only parses and SpacegroupAnalyzer is spglib in C. On a 32-atom P1 cell (an fcc supercell with 1 536 operations) the kit takes 361 ms against 13 ms, 28 times slower; on a four-atom cell they are level. Listed first in docs/open-items.md.

orientation_reduce: the kit reduces one matrix at a time (24 proper operators); orix reduces a vectorised batch

(Orientation.from_matrix + map_into_symmetry_reduced_zone). The kit is 2.5 to 12 times faster at these sizes.

ellipse_fit_1d: level with py4DSTEM's fit_ellipse_1D (0.94 and 1.1).

acom_library: level with orientation_plan for about 600 zone axes (0.97); py4DSTEM's count comes from its angular step

(630 at 1.6 degrees), the kit's from zoneAxisCount. 128 in-plane steps on both sides.

acom_match_scan: the kit's matchAll over the whole scan against py4DSTEM's match_single_pattern per pattern (one

core; Python's per-pattern time includes its own refinements, so this is a time, not a like-for-like algorithm). Per pattern the kit on one core is 14 times (8 x 8 scan) and 9 times (32 x 32) faster than py4DSTEM on one core, and on all cores 105 and 81 times.
